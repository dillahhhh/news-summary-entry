World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

IRAN WAR: U.S. Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris the U.S. economic campaign was devastating enough that "there's really no need for major combat operations any longer," though force remains possible if Iran "crosses certain red lines" (New York Times). Iran's security chief Mohsen Rezaei said Tehran still awaits a U.S. answer to its seven conditions for peace talks, and the Strait of Hormuz will not reopen until they are met. Three oil tankers caught fire after striking naval mines; monitors counted at least 37 attacks on ships near Hormuz in November, the highest monthly total since the war began.

RUSSIA-UKRAINE: Trilateral talks in Abu Dhabi ended with agreement on a large prisoner-of-war exchange, Russian negotiator Vladimir Medinsky said; President Zelenskyy called the talks "constructive and quite substantive" (Reuters). NATO scrambled jets after drones strayed into Baltic airspace during Ukrainian strikes on Russia.

GAZA: Israeli forces killed at least seven people in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, health officials said.

UNITED STATES: The House voted 211-204 to refer an impeachment resolution against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to the Judiciary Committee, effectively killing it. The Justice Department charged three Iran-backed operatives over alleged plots in the U.S. and Europe.

HEALTH: The Democratic Republic of the Congo confirmed 11,042 Ebola cases and at least 5,387 deaths; the Bundibugyo outbreak is the second largest on record.

CLIMATE: U.N. chief Antonio Guterres told leaders at COP31 in Turkey that the slow response to climate change had placed the world "on a path to destruction." Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu endorsed a plan to double homes with rooftop solar by 2030.

TECH: Nvidia's board hired a former federal prosecutor to examine CEO Jensen Huang's ties to an alleged Chinese spy, after a defense-linked Chinese university bought two H200 chips.
