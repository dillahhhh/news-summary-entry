World news, Wednesday 11 November 2026.

IRAN: Secretary of State Marco Rubio said in Paris the U.S. economic campaign was so devastating there was "no need for major combat operations" against Iran, but force remains possible if Tehran crosses "red lines" (Reuters). Tehran's Mohsen Rezaei said Hormuz stays shut until the U.S. meets its seven conditions for talks, and denied any talks are under way. Two tankers caught fire on mines, the Guards said; 37 ships were attacked near Hormuz in October, the war's worst month, and 10 more since (Washington Institute).

UKRAINE: U.S.-mediated Abu Dhabi talks agreed the war's largest prisoner swap, over 1,000 people within weeks, but no breakthrough on peace (Reuters). NATO jets scrambled overnight as unidentified drones entered Baltic airspace, origin unknown (Reuters).

GAZA: Israeli fire killed at least seven in Gaza, including a father and his 13-year-old son, medics said, despite the ceasefire (Reuters).

UNITED STATES: The House voted 211-204 to send Rep. Thomas Massie's impeachment articles against Defense Secretary Pete Hegseth to committee, effectively killing them. A Florida judge let Trump's $10bn BBC defamation suit proceed and dismissed a separate trade claim (Reuters).

MARKETS: U.S. prices rose 0.4% in October, above the 0.3% forecast; the S&P 500 fell 0.4% and the Nasdaq 0.7%, a December rate rise now fully priced (Reuters).

HEALTH: Congo's Ebola outbreak passed 11,000 cases: 11,042 confirmed, 5,387 dead, the second-largest outbreak on record (Reuters).

CLIMATE: U.N. chief Antonio Guterres told COP31 leaders in Antalya the slow climate response had put the world "on a path to destruction" (Reuters). Australia, Bangladesh, Kenya, Nigeria and Vanuatu backed doubling rooftop-solar homes to 300m by 2030 (AP).

TECH: Nvidia's board hired ex-U.S. attorney Joon H. Kim to probe CEO Jensen Huang's ties to Alice Huang, whom U.S. officials believe is a Chinese spy; it said no secrets were believed compromised (BBC).
